# Expect the copy draft to win on T4

The plan is sound in design, but its main hidden assumption is optimistic. On a Kaggle T4 in eager Hugging Face mode, both Qwen models are limited by per-step overhead rather than by memory bandwidth. As a result the 0.5B draft's cost ratio c is probably **about 0.5 to 0.7, not 0.3 to 0.6**, and the paper's formula then predicts that the neural draft will give **roughly 0.8× to 1.2×**, while prompt lookup is the only draft likely to show a clear speedup. Everything else in the plan will run, but seven concrete problems will break it quietly unless the plan changes. First, Qwen's shipped `generation_config.json` turns on sampling and a repetition penalty, which breaks any "greedy" baseline built on `generate()`. Second, the current transformers 5.x cache API changed what `crop()` means (in v5.15) and removed `cache_position` (in v5.4). Third, Kaggle now ships transformers 5.x on torch 2.10, so pinning a 4.x version would be a risky cross-major downgrade. Fourth, FlashAttention is not available on the T4, and Qwen2-family models have a documented float16 overflow history. Fifth, the plan's "counted" acceptance rate is a biased estimator. Sixth, randomly initialised tiny models will hardly ever exercise the rejection path. Seventh, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct is under a non-commercial research licence, not Apache-2.0. None of these threatens the project's premise. The plan already says a small or negative speedup is a complete result. But the plan should be edited now so that the answer is correct and the write-up is honest about why.

## The 0.5B draft costs about two-thirds of a 3B step, so it will likely break even at best

The strongest evidence comes from Qwen's own benchmark. In Hugging Face transformers at batch size 1 on an A100, **Qwen2.5-3B decodes at 30.8 tok/s and Qwen2.5-0.5B at only 47.4 tok/s**, and the 7B (40.4 tok/s) is actually *faster* than the 3B ([Qwen speed benchmark](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/v2.5/benchmark/speed_benchmark.html)). On the same GPU, vLLM runs the same models 4.1× to 6.6× faster. So HF eager decoding of small models is dominated by framework and kernel-launch overhead, and per-step time scales with layer count (24 against 36, a ratio of 0.67) much more than with parameter count. The layer counts are confirmed by the configs ([0.5B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json), [3B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json)). The implied c on the A100 is **30.8 / 47.4 ≈ 0.65**. The T4's slower memory makes the 3B's GPU time a larger share of its step, so c on Kaggle probably lands around 0.5 to 0.7. That value is an estimate; no T4 measurement for this pair was found. The plan's estimate of 20 to 25 tok/s for the 3B baseline (section 9) is plausible. A bandwidth floor of about 19 ms/token plus overhead on Kaggle's weaker CPUs gives an estimated 18 to 30 tok/s.

Putting c ≈ 0.65 into the plan's own formula (section 3) shows how little room the neural draft has. The speedup formula is (1 − α^(γ+1)) / ((1 − α)(γc + 1)).

| α | γ | c = 0.65 | c = 0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.7 | 4 | 0.77× | — |
| 0.8 | 1 | 1.09× | — |
| 0.8 | 4 | 0.93× | 1.53× |
| 0.9 | 2 | 1.18× | — |
| 0.9 | 4 | 1.14× | — |

The rows for α = 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 at γ = 4 match the analysis in the research notes. The γ = 1 and γ = 2 rows are computed directly from the formula. The pattern matters for experiment 5. At high c, **the best γ for the neural draft is 1 or 2**, and larger γ only makes things slower. The planned grid (1, 2, 3, 4, 6) covers this range, but the write-up should expect a peak at small γ. Slowdowns with larger pairs are already on record. On an A100, Llama-3.1-8B with a Llama-3.2-1B assistant ran at **31.1 ms/token against 24.4 ms/token for the baseline, about 28% slower** ([transformers #36337](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/36337)). HF's own guidance is that the assistant should be "at least an order of magnitude smaller" ([HF assisted generation blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/assisted-generation)). The 3B/0.5B pair is only 6× apart. The acceptance rate itself should look healthy. The only published Qwen2.5-0.5B-drafting result found (for a 7B target, on a CPU) reports **about 62% acceptance and 2.64× on MBPP** ([ML-SpecQD](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13565)). A high α combined with a flat speedup is exactly the "α > c" story that section 3 of the plan wants to tell.

The copy draft is where a real speedup is likely. Prompt lookup has c ≈ 0. On Spec-Bench (Vicuna-7B, RTX 3090) it gives **1.72× on GSM8K, 1.64× on MT-Bench conversation, and 1.64× overall**, with a mean of 1.73 accepted tokens per step ([Spec-Bench leaderboard](https://github.com/hemingkx/Spec-Bench/blob/main/Leaderboard.md)). Its original author reports about 2.4× on input-grounded tasks ([prompt-lookup-decoding](https://github.com/apoorvumang/prompt-lookup-decoding)). With 128-token outputs and short prompts there is less context to copy from, so a reasonable prior is about 1.2× to 1.7× on code and maths and near 1× on open chat. The plan's hypothesis in question 2, that the zero-cost draft beats the neural one, is therefore the likely outcome, and the formula can predict it. The verification-cost curve (experiment 4) should be close to flat for γ+1 ≤ 9, because the T4's ridge point is about 200 FLOP/byte and a k-token decode has an arithmetic intensity of about k. This is an estimate and should be measured. The same reasoning says that most of c is software overhead. That makes the deferred extension "compiled models (static cache with `torch.compile`)" in section 12 the most informative follow-up. HF documents **up to a 4× speedup** from static cache plus compile ([HF LLM optimisation docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/llm_optims)), which could push the draft's c toward 0.1 to 0.2.

## Qwen's shipped defaults quietly break greedy decoding and float16 safety

The plan is right that the two models are compatible. Both have **`vocab_size` 151936 and tied embeddings**, and their `tokenizer_config.json` files are byte-identical, so they share the same tokenizer and chat template ([3B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json), [3B tokenizer_config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/raw/main/tokenizer_config.json)). The check in `models.py` (section 4) will pass. It should still slice logits to the 151,665 real tokens (151,643 regular plus 22 control tokens, per the [Qwen2.5 technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15115)) before any softmax. The remaining 271 rows are padding and are not guaranteed to have zero probability. The slice also makes the section 12 extension to a 7B target work, because **Qwen2.5-7B has `vocab_size` 152064 and untied embeddings** ([7B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct/raw/main/config.json)). Without the slice, p/q operations for that pair would fail with a shape error.

The larger trap is `generation_config.json`. All the Instruct models ship **`do_sample: true`, temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8 and top_k 20**. They also ship a repetition penalty that **differs between the pair: 1.05 for the 3B and 1.1 for the 0.5B** ([3B generation_config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/raw/main/generation_config.json), [0.5B generation_config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct/raw/main/generation_config.json)). In transformers v5.17, `generate()` merges these values under the user's arguments. It also adds `RepetitionPenaltyLogitsProcessor` whenever the penalty is not 1.0, **whether or not `do_sample` is set** ([generation/utils.py v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/generation/utils.py)). A `generate(do_sample=False)` reference, including HF assisted generation in the "later extensions", would therefore differ from the hand-written argmax loop. The plan's baseline (section 8, `baseline.py`) must build distributions from raw `model(...).logits`. Any call to `generate()` must pass `repetition_penalty=1.0` explicitly. The stopping rule must include **both EOS ids, 151645 (`<|im_end|>`) and 151643 (`<|endoftext|>`)**. The chat template also has a quirk: it injects "You are Qwen, created by Alibaba Cloud. You are a helpful assistant." when no system message is given ([tokenizer_config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct/raw/main/tokenizer_config.json)). Both models see it, so fairness is unaffected, but it adds about 20 tokens to every prompt and should be documented.

Float16 is a real but manageable risk. The Qwen team's Qwen2 documentation says: "We don't recommend using `float16` for Qwen2 models or numerical instability may occur". The listed symptoms are `probability tensor contains either inf, nan` errors and repeated `!!!!` output ([Qwen docs v2.0](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/v2.0/_sources/inference/chat.md.txt)). Their patch shows the cause: the Q·Kᵀ product overflows in the eager path, **specifically in the last layer of 7B-shaped models** ([jklj077 qwen2-patch commit](https://github.com/jklj077/transformers/commit/53dba62c)). Current transformers' eager attention still scales *after* the fp16 matmul ([modeling_qwen2.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/models/qwen2/modeling_qwen2.py)). PyTorch's SDPA math path upcasts to fp32 and pre-scales q and k ([attention.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/main/aten/src/ATen/native/transformers/attention.cpp)). No report was found of the 0.5B or 3B failing in fp16, but that is absence of evidence, not proof. On the T4 specifically, **PyTorch's flash SDPA backend requires sm80 or newer, so it is unavailable; the memory-efficient backend (sm50+) and the math backend are available** ([sdp_utils.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pytorch/pytorch/main/aten/src/ATen/native/transformers/cuda/sdp_utils.cpp)). FlashAttention-2 does not support Turing at all ([flash-attention README](https://github.com/dao-ailab/flash-attention)). The plan should therefore state `attn_implementation="sdpa"` explicitly for both models and never use `flash_attention_2`. It should also check `torch.isfinite(logits)` on the baseline before blaming the speculative loop. This matters even more for the 7B extension, which has exactly the dimensions of the documented failure.

## Transformers 5.x changed the cache API underneath section 8

Section 8 assumes that `DynamicCache.crop()` rolls the cache back to the last accepted token. The method still exists, but **its argument changed meaning in v5.15.0: "Cropping can only be done with negative values"** ([v5.15.0 release](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/releases/tag/v5.15.0)). In v5.17, a positive argument logs a deprecation warning and is scheduled for removal in v5.18. `crop(0)` is even more dangerous. In v4.x through v5.13 it **silently empties the entire cache**; from v5.14 it is a no-op ([cache_utils.py v4.57.1](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v4.57.1/src/transformers/cache_utils.py), [issue #47433](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/47433)). The only form that works on every version is `if n_reject > 0: cache.crop(-n_reject)`. It should be followed by `assert cache.get_seq_length() == committed_len` every round. HF's own `_assisted_decoding` now calls `crop(-(candidate_length - n_matches))` ([generation/utils.py v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/generation/utils.py)). The assertion is the only reliable way to catch a rollback error. **v5.4.0 removed `cache_position` from model forward signatures** ([v5.4.0 release](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/releases/tag/v5.4.0)). Qwen2 now computes positions and the causal mask from `cache.get_seq_length()` ([modeling_qwen2.py v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/models/qwen2/modeling_qwen2.py), [masking_utils.py v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/masking_utils.py)). If stale tokens survive a rollback, RoPE positions and the mask shift together, and the output stays plausible. The only symptom is a greedy mismatch.

Three smaller API facts belong in the plan. First, a manual forward returns logits for **every** position unless `logits_to_keep` is set, and it does not upcast them to fp32. Prefill should pass `logits_to_keep=1`, because otherwise a 1,000-token prompt creates about 300 MB of fp16 logits, and all probability maths should run in fp32. Second, `StaticCache` has **no `crop` method** in v5.17 ([cache_utils.py v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/cache_utils.py)), so the compiled extension would need a hand-written rollback. Third, a fair HF assisted-generation comparison (a later extension) must set `num_assistant_tokens=γ`, `num_assistant_tokens_schedule="constant"` and `assistant_confidence_threshold=0.0` on the **assistant's** `generation_config`. The default threshold of 0.4 makes HF stop drafting early ([configuration_utils v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/generation/configuration_utils.py), [candidate_generator.py v5.17.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.17.0/src/transformers/generation/candidate_generator.py)).

The latest released Kaggle GPU image, **v170 (2026-06-29), ships torch 2.10.0+cu128, transformers 5.x and accelerate 1.13.0** ([Kaggle docker-python releases](https://github.com/Kaggle/docker-python/releases)). Its requirements specify only `transformers>=5.0.0` ([kaggle_requirements.txt](https://github.com/Kaggle/docker-python/blob/main/kaggle_requirements.txt)). A new base image moving to **Python 3.13 and torch 2.11** was merged on 2026-09-28 and has not been released yet ([PR #1562](https://github.com/Kaggle/docker-python/pull/1562), [PR #1557](https://github.com/Kaggle/docker-python/pull/1557)). The environment could therefore change during development, which makes section 4's advice to pin the environment timely. Section 8's "pin `transformers` exactly" should pin **a 5.x version (for example 5.17.0)**, not a 4.x one. Downgrading across a major version fights the preinstalled huggingface_hub 1.x and peft 0.19. The install must also happen before anything imports transformers, because "Save & Run All" cannot restart the kernel. The T4 itself is safe: sm_75 is kept in the cu128 and CUDA 13 builds ([PyTorch dev-discuss](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/notice-cuda-12-6-wheels-will-no-longer-be-published-from-pytorch-2-15-drops-maxwell-pascal-volta/3432)). The notebook should not reinstall torch.

The hardware menu changed in September. Kaggle staff confirmed that **single T4 is no longer offered; the GPU pool is T4 ×2** ([kaggle-cli #1196](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-cli/issues/1196)). The P100 was retired on 2026-09-15 ([Kaggle announcement](https://www.kaggle.com/discussions/product-announcements/735239)). Loading with `device_map="auto"` would shard the 3B across both GPUs and distort timings, so the timing runs in section 4 should use `device_map={"": 0}`. The float32 test in experiment 3 is feasible as planned: the 3B needs **11.5 GiB in fp32**, which fits on one T4 by itself. Logits must be moved to a common device before comparison. For downloads, Qwen2.5 is ungated. However, anonymous HF requests are **rate-limited per IP** (3,000 resolver calls per 5 minutes), and HF calls a missing `HF_TOKEN` "the number one reason users get rate limited" ([HF rate limits](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/rate-limits)). Put the token in Kaggle Secrets. `HF_HUB_ENABLE_HF_TRANSFER` is deprecated in favour of hf-xet ([huggingface_hub env vars](https://huggingface.co/docs/huggingface_hub/package_reference/environment_variables)). Cache weights outside `/kaggle/working` so that 7 GB of weights do not end up in the 20 GB output. Qwen2.5 is on Kaggle Models as `qwen-lm/qwen2.5/transformers/3b-instruct` ([Kaggle Models](https://www.kaggle.com/models/qwen-lm/qwen2.5/Transformers/3b-instruct)). Headless runs via `kaggle kernels push` need `machine_shape: "NvidiaTeslaT4"`, and there are two pitfalls. A `docker_image` digest left in `kernel-metadata.json` overrides the accelerator image, and it once sent a GPU request to `torch+cpu` ([kaggle-cli #1197](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-cli/issues/1197)). Retired shapes are also substituted without warning. The first cell should assert two CUDA devices named T4 and fail fast otherwise.

## The tests and the α estimator need sharper definitions

Section 6 defines the counted α as "accepted draft tokens divided by proposed draft tokens". Draft tokens after the first rejection are proposed but never evaluated, so this ratio estimates α(1 − α^γ)/(γ(1 − α)) rather than α. For example, **it gives about 0.55 when the true α is 0.8 at γ = 4**. That would systematically distort experiment 6. The paper defines α = E[Σ min(p, q)] (Corollary 3.6) and evaluated it over 10K target-generated tokens ([Leviathan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)). The counted estimator should divide by *evaluated* positions (accepted drafts plus the one rejected draft), and Σ min(p, q) should be computed at exactly those positions, never after a rejection. The two numbers should then agree within sampling error, and a gap would signal a bug. Prompt-lookup rounds with no match should be excluded and reported as a separate rate. Watch the notation too. Chen et al. swap the symbols (their q is the target), so code copied from that paper inverts the ratio ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)).

The acceptance-rule code needs three guards. First, compute p and q in fp32. Second, compare `r * q(x) < p(x)` instead of dividing. Third, when the residual max(0, p − q) sums to nearly zero, fall back to sampling from p. HF does no guard here, and vLLM adds a tiny value that it admits introduces "drift" ([vLLM rejection_sampler.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.6.3/vllm/model_executor/layers/rejection_sampler.py)). An unguarded NaN has turned into an out-of-vocabulary token in production ([vLLM #53029](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/53029)). For the copy draft in section 8, HF's approach is simpler and exact: sample y ~ p and accept only if y equals the proposed token. It avoids building a fake one-hot q ([HF v4.46.0 generation/utils.py](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v4.46.0/src/transformers/generation/utils.py)). EOS and `max_new_tokens` must be checked inside each accepted block, and tokens after them discarded. HF had to patch this case ([transformers #48039](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/48039)), and reference repositories overshoot by up to γ ([feifeibear speculative_sampling.py](https://github.com/feifeibear/LLMSpeculativeSampling/blob/main/sampling/speculative_sampling.py)). Overshoot would inflate tokens per second.

Experiment 2 needs one design fix. Randomly initialised models with the default `initializer_range` of 0.02 produce nearly uniform p and q, so α ≈ 1 and the residual path almost never runs. Raise the initialiser scale or lower the temperature so that p and q differ, and **require a minimum fraction of rounds that hit the rejection branch**. The plan's "classic rollback bug" (section 8) is real, but it is invisible to distribution tests: a hole in the draft cache lowers α without biasing the output. Add a direct test that compares cached next-token logits with a fresh no-cache forward after every round. For experiment 3 in float16, divergence is expected, not a defect. In a greedy Qwen2.5-32B/7B bf16 run, the first divergence came at logits tied at **24.125 vs 24.125**, and an HF maintainer said that "it is expected" because the verify pass uses different GEMM kernels ([transformers #39421](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/39421)). vLLM measured acceptance falling from about 100% to 72% purely from differences at the scale of one unit in the last place ([vLLM RFC #54506](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/54506)). Fix the near-tie margin threshold in advance. The strongest float16 check is teacher-forced: run the target once without a cache over the emitted sequence, and require every token to be its argmax or a logged near-tie. For timing (section 7), warm-up must cover every query length (1, γ+1 and the prefill). Log `nvidia-smi` clocks and temperature during runs, because the passively cooled 70 W T4 throttles ([Speechmatics CUDA timing guide](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/timing-operations-in-pytorch)).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the project's centre of gravity. The plan was written as "how much does speculative decoding speed up the 3B, and does the formula explain it?" On a free T4 in eager PyTorch, the more likely and more interesting finding is that **the neural draft's cost comes almost entirely from software overhead rather than hardware**. A 6× smaller model costs about two-thirds as much per step, so it cannot pay for itself, while a draft that costs nothing can. That outcome still tests the paper's theory, and it tests Corollary 3.9 (α > c) more sharply than a clean 2× speedup would. It also turns the "compiled models" extension from optional polish into the natural next experiment, because compiling is how to prove that c is overhead.

The remaining uncertainties should be settled in the first `--quick` run, not argued in advance: the real T4 value of c, the flatness of the verification-cost curve, whether the 3B shows any fp16 instability under SDPA, and which transformers version the Kaggle image actually provides. Every number in this review that has no T4 measurement behind it is an estimate. The plan's own experiment 4 is the right instrument for replacing each of them.
